# Visionary Sensing™

Aether’s Visionary Sensing™ technology revolutionizes greenhouse gas emissions management with a novel sensing technique that mimics the way our eyes perceive color. Our Visionary Sensor™ system detects and measures chemical gases from a distance without complex optics or lab-style equipment. Tuned for methane, our sensor offers precise detection and quantification of fugitive and enteric releases. Future versions will detect a range of organic and industrial chemical gasses, including chemical warfare agents.

## Our Mission

Aether was born from FedTech’s Homeland Security Startup Studio. From the start, our mission has been to develop better sensors to detect and quantify harmful chemical gasses that pose risks to our nation’s military and first responders. Since our founding, our mission has expanded to include chemical gasses that pose a risk not just to our nation, but to our planet – Greenhouse Gasses (GHG). Our mission is to:

- Save lives – recent studies estimate that between 5-8% of global deaths are attributable to anthropogenic (man made) greenhouse gas emissions. While it is difficult to estimate such numbers precisely, it is clear that any efforts to better detect GHG releases will have an immediate impact on global health and longevity.

- Improve the environment – Greenhouse Gasses are among the most potent drivers of global climate change. The planetary warming effect of accumulating GHG in the atmosphere has nearly doubled in the last 75 years. Cost-effective continuous monitoring of anthropogenic GHG emissions can help reverse the trend.

- Promote better policy – while the impact of increasing GHG emissions is better understood every day, effective policies to limit or reverse those impacts lag far behind. Better technology to more accurately detect and quantify GHG emissions at the source can be a powerful tool in crafting better policies at every level.

At Aether, we’re committed to developing Visionary Sensing™ technologies that help ensure life on Earth.

## Technology

The human eye is capable of discerning about a million colors. It does this by utilizing three types of cone cells that can each register about 100 shades of color, resulting in around one million combinations. Working with one of the U.S. Government’s premier national defense laboratories, Aether has developed a sensor that mimics the human eye’s ability to see color. We call this Visionary Sensing™. Our Visionary Sensors™ can literally “see” chemical gasses in outdoor air. This unique and innovative biomimetic approach results in a completely passive stand-off sensor that is ideally suited for continuous monitoring of GHG and other potentially harmful chemical gas emissions.

Our Visionary Sensor™ suite is dramatically less expensive than currently fielded monitoring equipment, easier to operate, easier to maintain and can be deployed in the harshest environments. Most of the emissions monitoring solutions in use today require a sensor carried by a person, aircraft or satellite to take a momentary snapshot in time. That snapshot is then aggregated with other similar snapshots to form an extrapolated assessment of the potential gas release. The problem is, the operator has to guess, or hope, that the snapshot is being taken at the right place and the right time to catch the emission and plot its source. Aether’s Visionary Sensors™ are cost-effective, SWAPC optimized, completely passive, and provide continuous monitoring.

## Use Cases

### Agricultural & Waste Management

Our Visionary Sensor™ optimized for methane detection and quantification is ideally suited for agricultural and waste management applications. Our small, rugged, and persistent sensor is designed with cattle operations, poultry farms, manure ponds, rice paddies and landfills in mind. We’ve designed our Visionary Sensor suite to be as close to “set it and forget it” as possible, eliminating many of the headaches and expense of currently available methane monitoring tools.

### Military & Security Services

On the modern battlefield and the front lines of homeland defense, our soldiers and security forces face unprecedented threats from chemical warfare agents, toxic industrial chemicals, pharma-based agents, and so-called “Novichuks” or fourth-generation agents. Stand-off chemical threat detection sensors available today are large, costly and require extensive training to operate. Aether’s Defense line of sensors have optimized SWAP (size, weight, and power) for military applications, along with significantly reduced cost of ownership. Our sensors are easy to use with minimal training or expertise required. Our sensors are compatible with several classes of small UAVs and robotic platforms. Our sensors can also easily be adapted to serve as unattended ground sensors for advanced warning of an approaching gas cloud.

### Industrial & Smart City Applications

Our sensors are easily adapted to a wide range of industrial and smart city uses. Our small and highly reliable sensors can form the basis of a distributed point cloud for advanced chemical detection to both warn of approaching hazards and monitor air quality for specific chemical markers. Our sensors can be used for compliance monitoring as well as anomaly detection in a variety of settings. We are easily integrated into existing IoT infrastructures.

## Barry Hess niche
Defense and intelligence technology companies in the $5M-$10M range whose proven prototype is stuck between demo and fielded deployment, guided by a CTO who fielded a patented tactical Android app platform with operators in theater and now engineers deployment architecture and acquisition advocacy from day one.

**EDP:** When months-to-field exceeds the funded months remaining on the contract, the program dies in the DoD valley of death - and for a $5M-$10M defense tech company, one dead transition is often the company.

## Deployed-reality checklist (score absence against this)
- **DDIL**: denied/disconnected/intermittent/low-bandwidth comms. Canonical example: an AI
  crowd-detection startup streamed 30fps video; the contested environment had bandwidth for
  1 frame every 5 seconds. One architecture change (frame-rate decoupling) saved the project.
- **SWaP-C**: size, weight, power, cost on the platform actually carried/mounted.
- **ATO/RMF**: Authority to Operate on government networks; STIGs, POA&Ms, IL4/IL5.
- **CMMC / NIST 800-171**: company-level compliance before the contract dies.
- **ITAR/export**: controlled data handling.
- **Operator training & sustainment**: who trains the E-5, who fixes it in the field, spares,
  battery logistics, GFE integration.
- **Advocacy chain**: a champion above the operator level; operators loving it is not adoption.
- **Prime/flow-down mechanics**: subcontract structure, government back-end integration.
- **TAK ecosystem**: if it touches situational awareness and never mentions ATAK/TAK, ask why.


## Doctrine
# Targeting doctrine brief (inject into every dossier & company agent — do NOT re-read the corpus)

Distilled from Jordan Crawford's Cannonball/Blueprint corpus, 2026-07-23.

1. **Pain-Qualified Segment beats ICP.** Never target firmographics ("50-200 person SaaS").
   Target companies in an observable painful SITUATION right now. The situation must be
   provable from public data.
2. **Existential Data Point (EDP).** For each niche, name the single number that kills the
   company if the problem stays unfixed (e.g. equipment utilization 60% vs 80% margins;
   fleet utilization <70% = crisis). Every dossier must state the EDP of its CTO's target
   situation: "what does it cost a company, in what metric, to leave this unfixed?"
3. **Jobs are confessions.** A job posting is a company publicly describing its own pain.
   We match jobs whose EXISTENCE signals a problem the CTO has already fixed — not jobs the
   CTO would apply to. "First Head of Engineering" = no technical leadership. "Integration
   Engineer (Epic, HL7)" = drowning in healthcare plumbing.
4. **The message is a redescription of the targeting.** If targeting is precise, the message
   writes itself by describing what we found. The reader should think "how do they know that?"
5. **Message-as-gift (GTM Shift letter standard).** Conversational, unhurried, 4–6 short
   sentences. Opens with a verified surprising fact. Every claim traces to a named public
   source. Valuable even if never answered. One light question, under 15 words. Bans: "I
   noticed", flattery, "hope you're well", meeting asks, personal details about the recipient
   as the opener. Lead with the CTO's expertise matched to the company's evident problem.
6. **Niche = Industry × Situation × Unique Angle** — all three specific (Ben Horowitz
   principle: not best botanist; best Japanese botanist specializing in Zen gardens).
7. **Insight tests**: counterintuitive (would other CTOs argue?), specific (exact tools/
   patterns), quantifiable (before/after numbers), demonstrable (showable in 5 minutes).
8. **Evidence honesty.** Actions > words > database records. Aggregator/API data (Blitz,
   FullEnrich) never corroborates itself — load-bearing claims need the person's or
   company's OWN public artifacts. Date every claim; label estimates as estimates.
9. **Dropping a bad fit is success.** Never pad a list to hit a count.
