## Niche statement (Industry × Situation × Unique Angle)

**Industry:** Low-voltage systems integrators and electrical contractors — plus the property-technology vendors serving multifamily, student housing, hospitality, and senior-living buildings — in the $2M–$20M revenue band (his own stated fit criteria).

**Situation:** The moment they try to add a new technology line — managed Wi-Fi, video surveillance, access control, smart-building IoT, service plans — on top of an install base that already trusts them, with ops and sales maxed out and **no senior technology leadership** to pick vendors, design the delivery model, and phase the rollout without breaking the core business. Observable tells: they're hiring a low-voltage division manager, a managed-Wi-Fi engineer, an estimator, or a "director of technology" they've never had.

**Unique Angle:** He has run this exact play from every seat — OEM (Control4, incl. the 4,300-room CityCenter deployment), proptech SaaS (K4Connect), integrator CTO (Groove, where the multifamily vertical he says he launched became the company's anchor), installer COO (Skyline Solar), and, by his own account, an electrical contractor who built the low-voltage division himself. His repeatable mechanics: vendor selection/onboarding/certification (300% Wi-Fi growth, 25% cost cut), self-serve estimators that unblock sales (weeks → minutes), and converting one-time installs into recurring service-plan revenue — delivered through a phased framework (S.C.A.L.E.) designed to not derail existing delivery.

**One sentence:** Fractional CTO for $2M–$20M low-voltage integrators and electrical contractors adding their next technology line (managed Wi-Fi, surveillance, access control, smart IoT) to buildings and customers they already serve — vendor strategy, estimating, and recurring-revenue delivery models, phased so the core business keeps shipping.

## The EDP (Existential Data Point)

**Recurring revenue ≈ $0 on an install base the company already owns.** In his own words (LinkedIn, 2026-06-02): "You finish your best month ever. Then the first rolls around and the calendar is empty again. I called it the treadmill." A project-only contractor restarts revenue at zero every month; one slow quarter means layoffs, and a business with no monthly recurring revenue sells for a fraction of the multiple of one with service contracts. Meanwhile the highest-margin, stickiest scope on their own job sites — network, Wi-Fi, cameras, service plans — is being handed to a competitor the GC will call directly next time (his 2026-06-03 post: "I was handing the best part of the job to a stranger"), and bids are lost to broader-line rivals (his site names this: "You've lost bids because your solution set was too narrow"). His published counterfactuals: +30% total revenue in under a year for the integrator who added managed Wi-Fi and surveillance; 300% YoY Wi-Fi growth in 6 months after vendor strategy; installs converted to service plans "that paid us for years." The kill metric: **months of payroll covered by contracted recurring revenue = 0.**
