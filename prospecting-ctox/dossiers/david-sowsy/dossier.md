Connected-hardware and medical-device companies (industry) at the moment their product's mobile companion app becomes the bottleneck — a BLE-device app that won't ship, a legacy iOS/Android codebase aging past OS and app-store deadlines, or a first companion app with nobody aboard who has shipped regulated mobile (situation) — served by a 30-year bare-metal-C-to-SwiftUI engineer who has personally shipped BLE medical apps, NIH-funded clinical data-collection suites, and solo App Store submissions, and who carries PMP-grade schedule discipline plus current local-LLM/CUDA chops (unique angle).

## The EDP (the number that kills)

**When the companion app dies in the store, new-device activations go to zero.** Google Play removes or hides apps that fall behind its annual target-API-level deadline, and Apple rejects updates built on outdated SDKs — so a hardware product that requires its app for setup and pairing loses 100% of new-customer activations, meaning 100% of new hardware revenue sits in the channel unactivatable until the app is rebuilt; for a regulated device the same slip stalls a cleared product that already burned its regulatory budget. One sentence: a connected device whose companion app misses a platform deadline or fails review stops selling entirely — not gradually — because every unit in the channel needs the app to become a product.
